GH, KH & pH: Research & Sources

This research record shows the studies, veterinary references, aquaculture guidance, technical documentation, and aquarium sources used behind The Tank Guide’s GH, KH, and pH content. It also shows where evidence is strong, where a source only partially supports a claim, and where historical citations could not be independently recovered.

What this research record covers

The Tank Guide assembled one master research package on GH, KH and pH in freshwater aquariums (compiled August 2026). It is a 94-page reference compilation, not an article. Its core idea: each parameter answers a different question — pH is how acidic the water is right now, GH is how much calcium and magnesium is dissolved, and KH is how much acid the water can absorb before pH moves. The package covers:

  • What pH measuresThe logarithmic scale, how pH is measured, and why it swings over a day.
  • General hardness (GH)Calcium and magnesium, units, and their roles in fish and invertebrates.
  • Carbonate hardness (KH) / alkalinityTerminology, units, and how KH is used up and replenished.
  • How GH, KH and pH relateCommon water profiles and why one number doesn’t predict the others.
  • Buffering and pH stabilityLow-KH systems, and a look at “stable pH matters more than the right pH” and “don’t chase pH.”
  • Soft-water and hard-water fishSpecies requirements, osmoregulation, and survival vs. thriving vs. breeding.
  • Source waterTap, well, softened and blended water.
  • RO/DI water and remineralizationWhy pure RO water isn’t aquarium water on its own.
  • Adjusting GH, KH and pHMethods, dosing arithmetic and risks — including driftwood, botanicals, crushed coral and aquasoils.
  • Planted tanks and CO₂The limitations of the common pH/KH/CO₂ table.
  • Nitrification and Old Tank SyndromeHow the nitrogen cycle consumes alkalinity, and gradual pH decline in older tanks.
  • Testing, units and conversionsIncluding TDS and conductivity.
  • Shrimp, snails and plantsIncluding where common shrimp parameter targets are hobby convention.
  • Care-sheet ranges, acclimation and mythsWhere commonly repeated numbers and claims come from, and how well they hold up.

This is a summary of the package’s scope. It is not a new set of care recommendations — for practical guidance, read the articles below.

How to read this research record

Direct support
The source directly supports the specific statement attributed to it.
Partial support
The source supports part of the statement, but the package wording is broader.
Background support
The source is relevant context but is not direct proof of the full claim.
Manufacturer documentation
The source verifies what a manufacturer says about its own product. It is not independent scientific validation.
Bibliography-only
The historical package listed the source but did not clearly connect it to a specific statement.
Historical attribution limited
The relationship was reviewed, but the surviving information is not sufficient for stronger attribution.
Citation mismatch
The cited source does not support the claim attributed to it.

Each source also carries a verification label: Verified; Verified · citation corrected (the source was confirmed, but details such as authors, journal, year or URL needed correcting); Verified · limited scope; or Could not be independently verified.

The audit is complete, but it did not resolve everything. These are the gaps that remain.

  1. Four bibliography sources could not be independently verified

    • Carbon dioxide (Tucker, C. S., 1984)
    • Accuracy of pH/KH relation for CO₂ (discussion thread)
    • pH relationship and the KH on a chart
    • Specialist shrimp-keeping resources (unnamed group)

    Tucker (1984) is cited in the verified SRAC Publication No. 464, which supports that it exists, but the original 1984 publication was not located. The two forum threads could not be recovered, and the shrimp-keeping resources were never named individually in the package.

  2. Timmons et al. (2018), Recirculating Aquaculture, was verified with a scope limitation

    The book’s bibliographic identity was verified, but the exact 4th-edition passage used for the nitrification stoichiometry (how much alkalinity nitrification consumes) was not directly inspected. The same figures are corroborated by the wastewater and aquaculture sources in this record, but this particular book-to-claim link is not treated as fully verified.

  3. Wurts (2002) does not support one claim attributed to it

    The package cited Alkalinity and Hardness in Production Ponds for a claim that swings in alkalinity may disturb hormonal regulation, gonad development and gamete production. Independent verification found that this article does not contain that reproductive mechanism. It discusses alkalinity, hardness, pH swings, stress, growth and pond productivity instead.

    The historical citation is preserved, but this specific claim should not be treated as externally supported by Wurts 2002. No replacement source has been substituted.

  4. The peat / atomic-absorption claim remains unresolved

    The package described an observation that peat-filtered water tests softer on a hardness titration while calcium measured by atomic absorption stays unchanged. That observation came from a hobby forum thread that could not be recovered, and it has not been traced to a peer-reviewed source. It should not be read as established evidence.

  5. Shrimp parameter ranges cannot be independently audited

    The package compared commonly cited shrimp GH, KH, pH and TDS ranges using an unnamed group of specialist shrimp-keeping resources. The individual sources did not survive in the package, so that numerical comparison cannot be checked. The package itself labelled these targets as hobby convention.

  6. Manufacturer documentation is not independent testing

    The Seachem Equilibrium and SaltyShrimp Bee Shrimp Mineral GH+ pages were verified as the manufacturers’ own documentation. That confirms what each company says about its product, including dosing figures — not that those claims were independently tested.

More broadly: not every source is peer-reviewed aquarium research. Much of the physiology research studies wild or model species, and aquaculture and wastewater figures come from ponds and treatment plants rather than glass tanks.

What we did not do

  • We did not silently rewrite historical citations. The original package wording is preserved alongside every correction.
  • We did not replace missing sources with convenient modern substitutes.
  • We corrected source details (authors, journals, years, URLs) in our research registry while keeping a record of what the original package said.
  • We did not upgrade a source’s standing just because it was on the right topic.
  • We did not hide unavailable references — they remain listed and marked as unavailable.

How this record was built

  1. The Tank Guide assembled a master GH/KH/pH research package for content development.
  2. The package’s bibliography was extracted into The Tank Guide’s research registry — 43 sources and 68 source-to-claim relationships.
  3. Every bibliography source received an independent verification result.
  4. Source-to-claim relationships were reviewed separately from the package’s own evidence labels.
  5. Corrections and unresolved citations were preserved rather than hidden.
  6. The audit was completed on September 25, 2026.
